4. Temperature vs ride volume¶

Daily aggregation: rides per day against the mean recorded temperature.

In [5]:
daily = (rides.dropna(subset=["temperature_c"])
          .groupby("date")
          .agg(rides=("ride_id", "count"), temp_c=("temperature_c", "mean")))
r = daily["rides"].corr(daily["temp_c"])
coef = np.polyfit(daily["temp_c"], daily["rides"], 1)

bins = pd.cut(daily["temp_c"], range(-10, 35, 5))
by_bin = daily.groupby(bins, observed=True)["rides"].mean()

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(13, 4.6))
axes[0].scatter(daily["temp_c"], daily["rides"], s=8, alpha=0.35)
xs = np.linspace(daily["temp_c"].min(), daily["temp_c"].max(), 50)
axes[0].plot(xs, np.polyval(coef, xs), color="#C44E52", lw=2,
             label=f"fit: {coef[0]:.1f} extra rides per 1 C")
axes[0].set_xlabel("mean daily temperature (C)")
axes[0].set_ylabel("rides per day")
axes[0].set_title(f"Daily rides vs temperature (Pearson r = {r:.2f})")
axes[0].legend()

labels = [f"{b.left:.0f}-{b.right:.0f} C" for b in by_bin.index]
axes[1].bar(labels, by_bin.values, color="#4C72B0")
axes[1].set_xlabel("mean daily temperature (5 C bins)")
axes[1].set_ylabel("mean rides per day")
axes[1].set_title("Mean daily rides by temperature band")
axes[1].tick_params(axis="x", rotation=45, labelsize=8)
for i, v in enumerate(by_bin.values):
    axes[1].text(i, v + 5, f"{v:.0f}", ha="center", fontsize=8)
fig.tight_layout()
fig.savefig(f"{CHARTS}/04_temp_vs_rides.png", dpi=130)
plt.show()

Bottom line¶

  • Demand peaks in the mid-summer weekday evening (see hour/day heat map) and is roughly 7x higher in the best month than the worst.
  • Ride length is highly right-skewed: median ~13 min, but ~95 rides ran for a day or more.
  • Central Square dominates both origins and destinations.
  • Daily volume tracks temperature tightly (r ~ 0.7): warm days bring far more riders.
  • Members are the majority of rides; casuals skew slightly longer and later.
  • Data quirks (parked bikes, charged members, loop rides) are small enough to keep in the clean set; details in FINDINGS.md.
