Peer Review: "Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding"

Venue: ICML (template: icml2023.sty, compiled with [accepted], main.tex:25) Reviewer files examined: main.tex, references.bib, main.bbl, figure1.png, alpha_v_tokens.pdf, alpha_v_optimal_gamma.pdf, arith_ops.pdf, trace_diagram.pdf

Summary of Contributions

The paper proposes speculative decoding for exact-acceleration autoregressive inference. A small approximation model $M_q$ greedily/sampling-generates $\gamma$ candidate tokens; the target model $M_p$ scores all $\gamma+1$ positions in one parallel pass; a novel acceptance rule (keep $x \sim q$ w.p. $\min(1, p(x)/q(x))$, on rejection resample from $norm(\max(0, p-q))$) guarantees the output distribution is exactly that of $M_p$. Contributions claimed: (1) speculative execution generalized to the stochastic setting ("speculative sampling"), (2) a decoding mechanism requiring no retraining, no architecture change, and no change to the output distribution. The analysis gives expected tokens per iteration $(1-\alpha^{\gamma+1})/(1-\alpha)$ under an i.i.d.-acceptance assumption, a walltime model with cost coefficient $c$, an optimal-$\gamma$ characterization, an arithmetic-ops cost model, and a "lenience" relaxation. Experiments: $\alpha$ measurements on a 97M GPT-like model, T5-XXL (EnDe translation, CNNDM summarization), and LaMDA 137B; walltime measurements only for T5-XXL vs. T5X at batch size 1 on a single TPU-v4, reporting 2.3X–3.4X speedups with T5-small as $M_q$.

Strengths

  1. The central algorithmic idea is simple, correct, and practically important. I verified the correctness proof (main.tex:646-677) and the multi-step extension: the acceptance identity $\beta = \sum_x \min(p,q)$ and the normalizer $1-\beta$ of $p'$ (main.tex:651) are correct, and the returned sequence is distributionally identical to autoregressive sampling from $p$ by induction over accepted prefixes.
  2. The paper is honest about scope. It reports configurations where the method underperforms (T5-large rows at 1.4X–2.2X, main.tex:490,493,497,500), states the i.i.d. assumption explicitly (main.tex:249), and acknowledges concurrent independent work (main.tex:600, chen2023accelerating).
  3. Internal numerical consistency is high. I recomputed every cell of Table 2 (main.tex:406-411) from the formulas in Theorems 3 and 5 with $c=\hat c=0$, and every Exp column of Table 6 (main.tex:712-724) from Theorem 3 with the listed $(\alpha,\gamma,c)$: all match to rounding. The bigram 1.25X claim (main.tex:450) also checks out exactly.
  4. The theory-vs-experiment comparison (Appendix C, main.tex:693-731) is the right analysis to include, and the trace diagram (trace_diagram.pdf) makes the concurrency argument concrete.
  5. The comparison with plain rejection sampling (Appendix B, main.tex:679-689) is correct and usefully establishes why the adjusted-distribution resampling matters: the naive accept rate $1/M \le \alpha$.
  6. The lenience bound ("no token sampled with probability greater than $p(x)/l$", main.tex:745) is correct; I verified it from the acceptance/rejection decomposition.

Weaknesses

  1. Walltime evidence is a single operating point. All speedups are batch size 1 on one TPU-v4 with T5 (main.tex:470). The paper's headline framing — "it may be a good default to accelerate sampling" (main.tex:188, main.tex:608) — is about production serving, where batched throughput (compute-bound) is the dominant regime and the extra $\gamma+1$-fold concurrent compute of main.tex:354 directly competes with larger batches. No batch $>1$ measurement is reported anywhere, and the limitation discussion (main.tex:608) only addresses "configurations where additional computation resources are not available" without data.
  2. The walltime cost model is load-bearing and only validated for $\gamma \le 7$. Theorem 3 (main.tex:330-339) assumes $\gamma+1$ concurrent $M_p$ evaluations cost exactly one evaluation (main.tex:320). This is a memory-bound idealization: per-step cost grows with $\gamma$ once attention/compute over $\gamma+1$ positions stops being free. Figure alpha_v_optimal_gamma.pdf consequently recommends $\gamma$ up to 24 (at $c=0.01$, $\alpha=0.9$), an extrapolation far outside the empirically validated range ($\gamma \le 7$, Table 1). The model has no term for this and the paper does not flag the extrapolation.
  3. Theory-vs-empirics gaps are attributed, not explained. Table 6 shows Exp 2.5 vs. Emp 1.7 and Exp 2.0 vs. Emp 1.4 (T5-large, EnDe) but Exp 2.4 vs. Emp 3.1 (T5-small, CNNDM, temp=0) — errors in both directions up to $\pm$0.7X. The two causes offered (main.tex:698-699: implementation optimization differences; i.i.d. violation) would bias in opposite, fixed directions, so they cannot both explain the pattern as stated. No measured tokens-per-iteration vs. predicted (eq:expected_num_tokens) is reported, which is the one number that would separate the two hypotheses.
  4. $D_{LK}$ is the total variation distance. Definition 2 (main.tex:269-272): $\sum_x |p(x)-\tfrac{p+q}{2}| = \tfrac12\sum_x|p-q| = TV(p,q)$. The "properties" corollary (main.tex:285-290) and Theorem 1 ($\beta = 1 - TV(p,q)$, main.tex:292-304) are textbook facts about TV distance and the optimal coupling; the paper presents them as a new divergence without a single sentence acknowledging the equivalence. The theoretical contribution is therefore thinner than the presentation implies.
  5. The lenience speedup numbers do not follow from the paper's own model. main.tex:753 claims 2.5X/3.1X/3.6X/5.0X for $l \in {1, 0.5, 0.3, 0.1}$ with $c=0.015$ and $\alpha \in {0.62, 0.71, 0.76, 0.84}$ (Table 7). Maximizing Theorem 3 over all integer $\gamma$ gives 2.33/2.94/3.45/4.79 — every claimed value is 5–8% above the theoretical maximum, and no $\gamma$ is stated. By contrast, the temp=0 lenience paragraph (main.tex:779) matches the model exactly at $\gamma=8, c=0.015$ (I recomputed 3.30/3.30/3.87/4.91). Whether the standard-sampling numbers are measured or computed is unstated.
  6. The "~60%" oracle-gain claim is unsupported. main.tex:439-440 claims the oracle-$\gamma$ improvement "can be up to ~60% higher" than fixed $\gamma$ "for typical values of $c$ and $\alpha$". Under the paper's own model, the ratio $[1/(1-\alpha)] / \max_\gamma \frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{(1-\alpha)(\gamma c+1)}$ ranges from 1.07X ($\alpha=0.5, c=0.01$) to 2.9X ($\alpha=0.9, c=0.1$); for the typical measured values ($c \approx 0.02$, $\alpha \approx 0.6$–$0.8$) it is 1.15–1.31X. The 60% figure is neither an upper bound nor typical; no derivation is given.
  7. Reproducibility is weak. No code or artifact is mentioned. No absolute latencies are reported (only ratios), the T5X baseline version is unpinned, generation lengths are not given, per-row $\gamma$ in Table 1 (values 3–7, main.tex:488-500) is never explained (tuned? from Figure 2?), $c$ is "estimated based on profiler traces" (main.tex:697) with no methodology, the 97M GPT-like model's training is unspecified beyond "trained on lm1b" (main.tex:515), and LaMDA results are internal-only. The algorithm itself is fully specified and trivially reimplementable (as chen2023accelerating demonstrated), which partially mitigates this.
  8. Shared-vocabulary requirement is never stated. Speculative sampling compares $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ pointwise (main.tex:204), so $M_p$ and $M_q$ must share a token space. This is only implicit in "same architecture ... and the same probability standardization" (main.tex:446). For a method pitched as a production default, the constraint (no cross-tokenizer draft models) should be explicit.
  9. Citation errors. main.tex:589 attributes "Wisdom of Committees" to \citep{Matching_Model_and_Instance_Complexities} (Schwartz et al., "The Right Tool for the Job"); the actual Wisdom of Committees paper (Wang et al. 2020) exists in references.bib:139-144 as Wisdom_of_Committees but is never cited (confirmed absent from main.bbl). Also, Generalized Aggressive Decoding (Xia et al. 2022), the direct lossless generalization of SAD, is in references.bib:41-47 (gad) but never cited, even though the SAD paragraph (main.tex:597-598) is exactly where it belongs. references.bib:49-55 (iad_maybe) duplicates the sad entry; references.bib:242-248 is unused.
  10. Figure 1 and text disagree on the example's length. main.tex:182 says the sentence "consist[s] of 38 tokens" generated in 9 serial runs; figure1.png shows 9 iterations but 32 tokens (31 generated plus [START]). The 9-runs claim and the first-line claim in the caption (main.tex:155: 4 accepted + 1 correction = 5 tokens) are consistent with the figure; the total is not.

Questions for the Authors

  1. How do the speedups change at batch sizes 2, 8, 32 on the same TPU-v4? At what batch size does speculative decoding become neutral or negative, and does that change the "good default" claim (main.tex:188)?
  2. How was the per-row $\gamma$ in Table 1 chosen? Were the reported numbers the best over a $\gamma$ sweep, and what is the sensitivity of the speedup to a mis-specified $\gamma$ (e.g., Table 2 suggests $\alpha=0.8, \gamma=2$ gives 2.44X vs. 3.69X at $\gamma=5$)?
  3. In Table 6, can you report measured tokens-per-iteration vs. the prediction of eq:expected_num_tokens? That would separate the i.i.d.-violation explanation from the "optimization differences" explanation (main.tex:698-699), which currently explain errors in opposite directions.
  4. What are the "optimization differences between our implementation and the baseline" (main.tex:698)? Is your implementation faster or slower than T5X per serial step, and by how much?
  5. Derive the "~60%" oracle improvement bound (main.tex:439-440). Under your own cost model I get 15–30% for typical $(c,\alpha)$ and up to 2.9X at $\alpha=0.9, c=0.1$.
  6. Are the lenience speedups at main.tex:753 measured or theoretical, and at what $\gamma$? They exceed the maximum of Theorem 3 at every $\gamma$ for the stated $c=0.015$.
  7. Did you verify exact string equality of outputs vs. T5X at temp=0, and how did you validate distributional identity at temp=1 (e.g., KL of empirical token frequencies over many samples)?
  8. For the $\alpha$ measurements (main.tex:513): are the 10K evaluation tokens free-running generations from $M_p$, and is $\alpha$ estimated on prefixes from $M_p$'s trajectory or $M_q$'s? The acceptance rate is prefix-distribution dependent, and during actual decoding the prefixes come from a mixture.
  9. Why no walltime results for the 97M GPT-like model or LaMDA, both of which are introduced as evaluation settings (main.tex:185)? For LaMDA, what is the dialog task/dataset, and how does the $Top_{40}$ filter footnote (main.tex:602) enter the $p$/$q$ standardization?
  10. What is the host-side overhead of the acceptance logic and the extra $M_q$ launches under XLA/TPU (op-by-op sync points), and is that part of the "implementation details" variance (main.tex:475)?
  11. Why does Figure 1 show 32 tokens where main.tex:182 claims 38?

Assessment

Novelty: good, not groundbreaking. The system contribution — exact-distribution speculative decoding for arbitrary stochastic sampling with off-the-shelf draft models — is a genuine step over Blockwise Parallel Decoding (greedy-only, trained drafts, main.tex:594-596) and SAD (copy-restricted, main.tex:597-598), and the near-simultaneous independent chen2023accelerating confirms the idea was ripe. The sampling primitive itself (accept w.p. $\min(1,p/q)$, resample from the residual $norm(p-q)$) is the classical residual-coupling trick, and $D_{LK}$ is exactly TV distance (Weakness 4), so the theory is a competent packaging of known facts rather than new mathematics. The negligible-cost draft analysis (bigram $\alpha \approx 0.2$, main.tex:450) and the lenience variant are genuinely new and useful observations.

Soundness: proofs sound, empirical base narrow. Every derivation I checked is correct (correctness appendix, Theorems 1/3/5, Corollary 1, rejection-sampling comparison, lenience bound), and Tables 2 and 6 are arithmetically consistent with the formulas. The weak points are the unvalidated free-concurrency assumption beyond $\gamma \le 7$ (Weakness 2), the unexplained two-sided theory-empirics gaps (Weakness 3), the unreproducible 60% and lenience numbers (Weaknesses 5–6), and walltime evidence confined to one model family, one hardware config, batch 1 (Weakness 1).

Clarity: strong. Well-motivated, well-structured, figures match claims (I verified alpha_v_tokens.pdf against eq:expected_num_tokens and arith_ops.pdf against Theorem 5). Minor blemishes: notation slip in Algorithm 1 line 226 (acceptance test should read $r_i > p_i(x_i)/q_i(x_i)$, free $x$), typos "anaylsis" (main.tex:320), "autogreressive" (main.tex:456), "sparcification" (main.tex:588), "CCN/DM" vs. "CNNDM" (main.tex:470), duplicated \appendix (main.tex:638,641), the \stepcounter{footnote}/\footnotetext hack spanning sections (main.tex:524-527,602), and the layout hack at main.tex:460.

Reproducibility: below bar for the experiments, fine for the algorithm. No code, no absolute timings, unpinned baseline, unspecified $\gamma$ selection and $c$ estimation, internal LaMDA setup (Weakness 7). An external team can reimplement the algorithm from Section 3 alone, but cannot reproduce Table 1 from the paper.

Are the Abstract's Claims Supported?

  1. "decoding $K$ tokens takes $K$ serial runs" (main.tex:144) — accurate.
  2. "sample ... faster without any changes to the outputs" (main.tex:145) — supported in distribution (Appendix A proof is correct); for temp $> 0$ individual samples differ from a baseline run, so "outputs" should read "output distribution". Partially supported as literally worded.
  3. "by computing several tokens in parallel" — supported by Algorithm 1 and the trace diagram.
  4. "hard tasks include easier subtasks approximated by efficient models" (main.tex:147) — supported by the $\alpha$ measurements (Table 3: 0.5–0.9 for drafts two orders of magnitude smaller; 0.03–0.23 for n-grams), which also usefully show the effect is graded rather than binary.
  5. "accelerate existing off-the-shelf models without retraining or architecture changes" (main.tex:149) — supported: existing T5 checkpoints used (main.tex:470).
  6. "demonstrate it on T5-XXL and show a 2X-3X acceleration compared to the standard T5X implementation, with identical outputs" (main.tex:150) — supported only under conditions absent from the abstract: batch size 1, single TPU-v4, the best draft model (T5-small), $\gamma$ tuned per row. Range is 2.3X–3.4X for that configuration; other tested drafts give 1.4X–2.2X. "Identical outputs" is exact for greedy, distributional for sampling. The claim survives with these caveats, but the abstract should say "at batch size 1" and "output distribution".
  7. The intro's framing that the method "may be a good default" (main.tex:188) is not supported by the experiments as presented, since the only measured regime (batch 1) is the method's best case and the throughput regime is unmeasured (Weakness 1).

Recommendation

Accept. The algorithm is correct (independently re-verified), the exactness guarantee is genuinely valuable and distinguishes the work from all prior adaptive/parallel decoding methods, the analysis is mostly self-consistent (I checked every table against the formulas), and the practical impact is real and already corroborated by independent follow-up work. The main gaps — batched-serving evidence, the unvalidated large-$\gamma$ extrapolation, the two unreproducible numbers (lines 439-440 and 753), the TV-distance mislabeling, and the citation errors — are fixable in a revision and do not undermine the core claims.

Score: 7/10 (Accept). Confidence: 4/5 — I verified all proofs and recomputed all tabulated theory numbers myself; my uncertainty concerns the implementation details behind the walltime measurements, which are internal to Google and could not be checked from the source.
