Peer Review: "Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding"

Reviewed source: main.tex (802 lines), references.bib (273 lines), main.bbl (245 lines), plus figure files (figure1.png 2624x657, alpha_v_tokens.pdf, alpha_v_optimal_gamma.pdf, arith_ops.pdf, trace_diagram.pdf). All equations and tabulated numbers below were re-derived and re-computed independently from the stated inputs.

1. Summary of contributions

  1. Speculative sampling (Sec. 3.3, main.tex:204): sample a guess $x \sim q(x)$, keep it if $q(x) \le p(x)$, accept it with probability $p(x)/q(x)$ otherwise, and on rejection resample from the normalized residual $p'(x) \propto \max(0, p(x) - q(x))$. The output is exactly distributed as $p(x)$ (proof in Appendix A, main.tex:649-677).
  2. Speculative decoding (Algorithm 1, main.tex:211-236): draw $\gamma$ guesses autoregressively from a cheap draft model $M_q$, verify them with a single parallel batch of $\gamma+1$ target-model ($M_p$) calls, and emit $n+1 \in [1, \gamma+1]$ tokens per iteration. No retraining, no architecture change, exact output distribution.
  3. Closed-form analysis (Sec. 4): acceptance rate $\beta = 1 - D_{LK}(p,q)$ where $D_{LK}$ is a symmetric divergence (Def. 2, main.tex:269-272); expected tokens per iteration $(1-\alpha^{\gamma+1})/(1-\alpha)$ under i.i.d. $\beta$ (Eq. 1, main.tex:251-254); walltime improvement $(1-\alpha^{\gamma+1})/((1-\alpha)(\gamma c+1))$ (Thm 5, main.tex:330-334); arithmetic-op inflation (Thm 7, main.tex:363-366); optimal-$\gamma$ procedure (main.tex:387-389).
  4. Experiments (Sec. 5): 2.3X--3.4X walltime speedups vs T5X for T5-XXL (11B) on WMT EnDe and CNNDM (Table 1, main.tex:477-507); empirical $\alpha$ across GPT-like 97M, T5-XXL, and LaMDA 137B targets with transformer, unigram and bigram drafts (Table 3, main.tex:526-585); theory-vs-empirics table (Table 4, main.tex:701-731); a "lenience" relaxation (Appendix E).

2. Strengths

  • The correctness proof is short, elementary, and correct. I re-derived it: the accept path contributes $\min(q, p)$ and the reject-resample path contributes $p - \min(p,q)$ (using the normalizer $1-\beta$, main.tex:651), summing to $p$. The guarantee holds for any draft $q$, including disjoint support (main.tex:444), and the argument extends cleanly to top-k/nucleus/temperature via the standardization reduction (Sec. 3.2, main.tex:200).
  • Every closed-form result checks out. I recomputed all of Table 2 (main.tex:406-411) exactly (e.g., $\alpha{=}0.9, \gamma{=}10$: ops $1.603$, speed $6.862$); the "Exp" column of Table 4 matches Thm 5 to two decimals in all 12 rows; the bigram claim "1.25X with $\gamma=3$" reproduces exactly ($(1-0.2^4)/0.8 = 1.248$, main.tex:450,522); the temp-0 lenience numbers at $\gamma=8$ (3.3/3.3/3.9/4.9X, main.tex:779) reproduce to two decimals from Table 5's $\alpha$ values.
  • Training-free, deployment-friendly design with off-the-shelf drafts (T5-small/base/large checkpoints), and an honest accounting of the price paid: increased arithmetic ops (Thm 7) and an explicit compute-availability precondition (main.tex:320, 354-357, 608).
  • Breadth of $\alpha$ measurement (Table 3): four model families, two temperatures, transformer and n-gram drafts, showing $\alpha \in [0.5, 0.9]$ for $\sim 100$--1000x smaller drafts and even $\alpha \approx 0.2$ for a trivial bigram on translation.
  • Good experimental hygiene where it matters most: a dedicated theory-vs-empirics table with the cost coefficient $c$ estimated from profiler traces (Appendix C), ablations over draft size, and both sampling regimes.
  • Candid positioning against the two closest prior methods (Stern et al. 2018, SAD) with concrete distinguishing properties (main.tex:594-598), and acknowledgment of concurrent independent work (Chen et al. 2023, main.tex:600).

3. Weaknesses

W1. Reproducibility is the paper's weakest axis. No code, data, or configuration release; single hardware (TPU-v4), batch size 1 only (main.tex:470); no absolute runtimes (tokens/s), no P50/P99; no T5X version, XLA flags, or numeric precision for the baseline; the LaMDA and 97M-GPT $\alpha$ measurements give no prompt set, context lengths, or generation lengths (main.tex:513,517-518); no error bars or confidence intervals on any $\alpha$ (10K tokens is fine for $\alpha \approx 0.8$ but the paper never states the sampling protocol or seeds). A reader cannot reproduce any number in Tables 1--5.

W2. The i.i.d. $\beta$ assumption is load-bearing but empirically untested. Eq. 1, Thm 5, Thm 7 and the optimal-$\gamma$ analysis all assume i.i.d. acceptance (main.tex:249), yet $\beta_{x_{<t}}$ is a function of the prefix and is certainly not independent across positions (long repeated segments, code, lists). The only acknowledgment is "being only an approximation" (main.tex:699). The paper could have measured per-position $\hat\beta$ (it already computes everything needed for Table 3) and reported its variance/autocorrelation and the gap between predicted and observed tokens-per-iteration. That direct test is missing.

W3. Several specific numerical/technical blemishes (details in Sec. 6): - the lenience acceptance inequality at main.tex:779 has the wrong direction (see L779 comment); - the temp-1 lenience speedups (2.5/3.1/3.6/5X, main.tex:753) exceed the maximum of the paper's own formula (2.33/2.94/3.45/4.79X) given Table 5's $\alpha$ and $c=0.015$; the $\gamma$ values used are not reported; - the "up to $\sim$60% higher" oracle-$\gamma$ claim (main.tex:440) is never derived and is not a consequence of the stated assumptions (under "unbounded compute" the fixed-$\gamma$ optimum already approaches the oracle value; my recomputation gives 0--114% uplift depending on unreported $(\alpha, c)$); - the encoder upper bound at main.tex:373 is vague (see L373); - "identical outputs" (abstract, main.tex:150; intro, main.tex:185) overstates the stochastic case — what is guaranteed is an identical distribution, which the body itself says correctly (main.tex:148,181,608).

W4. Theory-vs-empirics agreement is weaker than "mostly match" (main.tex:698). Two configurations show the empirical speedup below the predicted value by 30-32% (EnDe T5-large: 1.7X vs 2.5X, 1.4X vs 2.0X, main.tex:714,717), while CNNDM T5-small/T5-base show +29%/+22% above (main.tex:719,723). The stated causes ("optimization differences", i.i.d. assumption, main.tex:699) are asserted, not quantified.

W5. Related work is thin for a camera-ready. references.bib itself contains entries that are never cited: Generalized Aggressive Decoding (Xia et al. 2022, references.bib:41-47) — a direct lossless-translation speedup method using a smaller model — and Adaptive Computation Time (Graves 2016, references.bib:242-248). Also, $D_{LK}$ is exactly the total-variation distance ($D_{LK} = \tfrac12\sum_x |p-q|$), so Thm 4 is the statement $\beta = 1 - \mathrm{TV}(p,q)$; this identification is never made, which overstates the novelty of the divergence bookkeeping and hides a useful connection to known rejection-sampling theory.

W6. Walltime evidence is confined to one model family and one hardware. Only T5 (encoder-decoder) on TPU-v4 has measured speedups; the decoder-only 137B LaMDA and 97M GPT results are $\alpha$-only (main.tex:513,517-522). The "good default" framing (main.tex:187-188,606-608) is broader than the measured evidence, and the KV-cache memory overhead of running $\gamma+1$ sequences in parallel is never quantified.

4. Questions for the authors

  1. (main.tex:779) Is "accept the token $x$ sampled from $M_q$ in case $p(x) \le l \cdot \max(p)$" a sign error? As written, smaller $l$ makes the rule stricter and rejects the argmax token itself ($p(x)=\max(p) > l\max(p)$ for $l<1$), the opposite of lenience. The monotone $\alpha$ values 0.75/0.75/0.80/0.87 for $l = 1/0.5/0.3/0.1$ are consistent with $p(x) \ge l\cdot\max(p)$. Please confirm and fix.
  2. (main.tex:753) Which $\gamma$ produces the reported 2.5X/3.1X/3.6X/5X? With the Table 5 $\alpha$ values and $c=0.015$, Thm 5 maximizes at 2.33X/2.94X/3.45X/4.79X over all $\gamma \in [1, 60]$; the reported figures require either $c \to 0$ or a larger $\gamma$ than any optimum. Please report $\gamma$ and the $c$ actually used, or reconcile.
  3. (main.tex:440) Please derive the "$\sim$60% higher" oracle-$\gamma$ bound. Under the stated "unbounded compute" assumption ($c \to 0$) the fixed-$\gamma$ optimum already equals $1/(1-\alpha)$ in the limit, so the stated identity $E(#\text{tokens}) = 1/(1-\alpha)$ for the oracle equals the trivial bound; the 60% figure seems to depend on specific $(\alpha, c)$ values that are not given.
  4. (main.tex:540,543 vs main.tex:522) The GPT-like 97M row has $\alpha(t{=}1)=0.89 > \alpha(t{=}0)=0.88$, contradicting "for all models, the sharper the adjusted distribution, the higher the $\alpha$ values". Is this within the measurement noise? What is the standard error of the 10K-token $\alpha$ estimates?
  5. (main.tex:470) The draft sizes "T5-small (77M), T5-base (250M), T5-large (800M)" do not match the sizes tabulated in the cited T5 paper (Raffel et al., 60M/220M/770M). Are these the T5 1.1 checkpoints? Please state exactly which checkpoints were used.
  6. (main.tex:714,717) What specifically explains the EnDe T5-large results falling 30-32% below the theoretical prediction (1.7X vs 2.5X; 1.4X vs 2.0X)? Is the $c=0.11$ estimate reliable?
  7. Can you report the empirical distribution of per-position $\hat\beta$ (variance, autocorrelation) and the gap between predicted and observed tokens-per-iteration, to validate the i.i.d. assumption?
  8. (main.tex:589) "Wisdom of Committees" is cited to Matching_Model_and_Instance_Complexities (Schwartz et al. 2020); the intended reference appears to be Wang et al. ICLR 2020 (references.bib:139-144, present but uncited, absent from main.bbl). Please fix the key.
  9. (main.tex:745) Please give the exact form of the output distribution under lenience and a proof of the guarantee $P_l(x) \le p(x)/l$. Note that for $l \ne 1$ the residual normalizer is $\sum_x (p(x)-lq(x))_+$, which is not $1-\beta_l$ (unlike the $l=1$ case used in Appendix A, main.tex:651), so the Appendix A argument does not transfer verbatim.
  10. (main.tex:697) How were the $c$ values estimated from profiler traces, and what is their uncertainty?
  11. (main.tex:470) What is the T5X baseline version and optimization configuration (JAX/XLA flags, bf16?), and what are absolute tokens/s numbers for both baseline and speculative decoding?

5. Assessments

Novelty — Moderate-to-high (system level), modest (sampling rule). The sampling rule itself is a classical rejection-sampling identity: the accept probability is $\min(1, p/q)$ and the "resample from $p-q$" residual makes one evaluation of $p$ amortize over up to $\gamma+1$ tokens; equivalently $\beta = 1-\mathrm{TV}(p,q)$, which the paper presents as a new divergence ($D_{LK}$) without naming the connection. What is genuinely new is (a) the batched parallel verification structure that turns one target-model call into $1$--$\gamma+1$ tokens, (b) the training-free off-the-shelf-draft formulation with an exact-distribution guarantee, and (c) the full $\alpha/c/\gamma$ analysis. Prior work (Stern et al. 2018 blockwise decoding; SAD) covers only greedy decoding and/or copy-style drafts with custom training, so the gap to "general stochastic, lossless, no training" is real and was not closed before this paper. Chen et al. 2023 arrived independently with the same core algorithm, which the paper properly acknowledges (main.tex:600) — it confirms novelty of the idea space but weakens first-publication credit for the algorithm itself.

Soundness — Good, with caveats. All proofs I re-checked are correct (Appendix A; Lemma 1; Thms 4-7; the $\gamma=1$ lower bound in the Corollary at main.tex:343-349 follows since the improvement factor is quasi-concave in $\gamma$, so $\alpha > c \iff f(1) > 1$). The main soundness caveats: (i) the i.i.d. $\beta$ assumption is untested (W2); (ii) the walltime model assumes $\gamma+1$ parallel target calls cost one serial call (main.tex:320), which is an assumption, not a property of TPU execution of a batched kernel — the 30% negative deviations in Table 4 show it can fail in either direction; (iii) the lenience appendix states a guarantee without proof and contains the sign issue (W3); (iv) the "60% oracle" and encoder-bound claims are underived/vague. None of these affects the core result (exact distribution + measured 2.3-3.4X speedup).

Clarity — Good. The paper is well-organized and the algorithm is presented compactly; the trace diagram (main.tex:430-436) and Figure 1 help. Dragged down by: template residue ("Llama specific" block main.tex:74-79, \COMMENTLLAMA at main.tex:209, "ICML 2023 EXAMPLE" comment at main.tex:1), a fragile \stepcounter{footnote} hack (main.tex:524), a duplicated \appendix (main.tex:638,641), a table label containing a space (tbl:empirical alphas, main.tex:528), mixed straight/curly apostrophes, and several typos (anaylsis main.tex:320, autogreressive main.tex:456, sparcification main.tex:588, down-stream main.tex:596). The abstract's "identical outputs" is imprecise relative to the body (W3).

Reproducibility — Weak. See W1. Everything is Google-internal (TPU-v4, LaMDA checkpoints, T5X codebase), no artifact is released, no absolute timings or variance are reported, and the $\alpha$-measurement protocol (prompts, lengths, tokenizer for LaMDA) is underspecified. For the era and venue this was common for systems results, but as written the experiments cannot be independently reproduced.

7. Are the abstract's claims supported?

Claim (abstract) Supported? Evidence
Decoding $K$ tokens takes $K$ serial runs Yes (motivation) Standard autoregressive decoding; no experiment needed
Faster sampling "without any changes to the outputs" / "identical outputs" Partially Exact-distribution guarantee is proven (Appendix A) and holds for both temp regimes (Tables 1, 3); but for stochastic sampling individual outputs are not identical — only the distribution is. "2X-3X ... with identical outputs" (L150) is precise only for the argmax rows.
"2X-3X acceleration compared to the standard T5X implementation" Yes (conservative) Table 1 measures 2.3X-3.4X (main.tex:488-500) vs T5X on TPU-v4; the abstract's range understates the best result.
"hard tasks include easier subtasks approximated by more efficient models" Yes $\alpha \in [0.5, 0.9]$ with $\sim$100-1000x smaller drafts across 3 tasks/4 model families (Table 3); even n-gram drafts give $\alpha \ge 0.16$ (L560-578).
"accelerate existing off-the-shelf models without retraining or architecture changes" Yes (with scope caveat) All speedups use existing checkpoints (L470); caveat: measured walltime is only for T5-XXL on TPU-v4 at batch size 1 — LaMDA/97M-GPT are $\alpha$-only (W6).
"novel sampling method" Yes The batched-verification + residual-resampling combination with the exact-distribution guarantee and full analysis is new relative to Stern et al. 2018 and SAD (L594-598); concurrent Chen et al. 2023 acknowledged (L600).

Verdict: the headline quantitative claim is supported (even understated); the "identical outputs" phrasing is the one substantive imprecision.

8. Recommendation

Accept (minor revisions). The core algorithm is elegant, the distributional guarantee is proven correctly, all closed-form results reproduce exactly from the paper's own inputs, and the measured 2.3X-3.4X speedups over an optimized T5X baseline with training-free off-the-shelf drafts constitute a strong, practically important contribution that was the first to combine losslessness, general stochastic sampling, and no training. The deficiencies are real but non-fatal: reproducibility is limited (W1), the i.i.d. assumption is untested (W2), and a handful of specific errors (sign at L779, under-derived 60% claim at L440, lenience numbers at L753, wrong citation key at L589) should be fixed. I verified the mathematics line by line; the errors above do not touch the central theorem or the main experimental comparison.

Confidence: 4/5 (high). I read the complete LaTeX source, bibliography, and figure inventory, and independently re-derived every equation and re-computed every tabulated figure (Tables 2, 4, 5, 6 and all closed-form spot checks); I did not run the models or inspect the raster figure's pixel content.
