Peer Review: "Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding"

Venue: ICML (source uses the ICML 2023 template, main.tex:25) Reviewer role: full technical + experimental review of LaTeX source (main.tex, references.bib, main.bbl, all figure files)

1. Summary of contributions

The paper proposes speculative decoding: use a cheap approximation model $M_q$ to autoregressively draft $\gamma$ candidate tokens, then evaluate all $\gamma+1$ positions in one parallel pass of the expensive target model $M_p$. A novel speculative sampling rule (main.tex:204, Algorithm 1 at main.tex:211-236) accepts each draft token $x_i \sim q_i$ with probability $\min(1, p_i(x_i)/q_i(x_i))$ and, upon the first rejection, resamples from the residual distribution $norm(\max(0, p-q))$. The construction provably preserves the target distribution exactly (Appendix A, main.tex:646-677). The paper derives:

  • expected tokens per target-model call under an i.i.d. acceptance assumption: $(1-\alpha^{\gamma+1})/(1-\alpha)$ (Eq. 1, main.tex:251-254);
  • $\alpha = 1 - D_{LK}(p,q) = E[\min(p,q)]$ (Thm. 1, main.tex:292-311);
  • expected walltime improvement $(1-\alpha^{\gamma+1})/((1-\alpha)(\gamma c + 1))$ (Thm. 2, main.tex:330-339) and an arithmetic-ops increase factor (Thm. 3, main.tex:363-370);
  • guidance for choosing $\gamma$ (main.tex:387-389, Fig. 3);
  • experiments: walltime speedups of 2.2X–3.4X for T5-XXL vs. T5X (Table 1, main.tex:477-507), and $\alpha$ measurements for GPT-like 97M, T5-XXL, and LaMDA 137B pairs (Table 3, main.tex:526-585);
  • appendices on correctness, comparison to rejection sampling, theory-vs-experiment, beam search, and a "lenience" relaxation.

Two claimed novel contributions (main.tex:190): (1) speculative execution generalized to the stochastic setting via speculative sampling; (2) a decoding mechanism that accelerates off-the-shelf autoregressive models with no retraining, no architecture change, and an unchanged output distribution.

2. Strengths

  1. The core algorithm is correct, simple, and genuinely useful. The speculative-sampling identity $\min(p,q) + (p-\min(p,q)) = p$ (main.tex:655-677) is a tight, exact coupling; the appendix proof is complete and the normalizing constant $1-\beta$ is correctly derived (main.tex:651). The guarantee holds for any $M_q$ (main.tex:444), which is a strong and practically valuable property that distinguishes it from Blockwise Parallel Decoding (Stern et al. 2018) and Shallow Aggressive Decoding.
  2. The theory-experiment loop is closed, not decorative. Table 4 (main.tex:701-731) plugs measured $\alpha$ and profiler-estimated $c$ into Thm. 2 and compares against measured walltimes; most rows agree within ~15% (e.g., EnDe T5-small t=0: 3.2 predicted vs 3.4 measured). I verified Eq. 1 and Thm. 2/3 algebra independently and they are correct; the bigram example at main.tex:450 ($\alpha=0.2, \gamma=3 \Rightarrow 1.248X$) checks out numerically, as do the Table 2 entries (main.tex:395-418) against Thm. 2/3 with $c=\hat c=0$.
  3. The rejection-sampling comparison (Appendix B, main.tex:679-689) is a genuinely useful clarification: the acceptance rate of naive rejection sampling is $1/M = \min_x q(x)/p(x)$-based and provably $\le \alpha$. The inequality chain is correct.
  4. Honest positioning of prior work. main.tex:594-600 explicitly differentiates from Blockwise Parallel Decoding and SAD, and acknowledges the concurrent independent work of Chen et al. 2023 (chen2023accelerating).
  5. Figure 1 is verifiable and consistent with the text. I checked figure1.png: 9 rows = 9 serial target-model runs; the first row generates 5 tokens (4 accepted drafts + 1 correction), matching the caption at main.tex:155. The trace diagram (trace_diagram.pdf) is consistent with the caption at main.tex:430-436.
  6. The cost model cleanly separates the two currencies (latency vs. arithmetic ops, main.tex:316-375), and the memory-access argument (main.tex:375) correctly identifies why the method helps in the memory-bandwidth-bound regime.

3. Weaknesses

Major

W1. The central walltime assumption — that $\gamma+1$ parallel $M_p$ evaluations cost the same as one — is never measured. main.tex:320 simply assumes it ("we'll assume that we can run $\gamma+1$ concurrent evaluations of $M_p$ in parallel without increasing the walltime"). This is the load-bearing assumption of the entire method, yet there is no plot of marginal walltime vs. $\gamma$ for fixed $\alpha$ (i.e., forced rejections), no measurement of where the "free parallelism" breaks down, and the trace diagram (trace_diagram.pdf) is explicitly "simplified." The paper's own data hints at the problem: for T5-large, predicted 2.5X vs. measured 1.7X (EnDe, t=0) and 2.0X vs. 1.4X (EnDe, t=1) in Table 4 — a 30–40% gap blamed vaguely on "optimization differences" and the i.i.d. assumption (main.tex:699). A direct measurement of parallel-evaluation cost vs. $\gamma$ would resolve this and is essential.

W2. Missing control experiment: the authors' own implementation without speculation. The speedups in Table 1 are measured against T5X, but the speculative implementation is custom. Since measured speedups exceed theoretical predictions in several rows (3.4 vs 3.2; 3.1 vs 2.4 for CNNDM T5-small t=0), part of the reported 2X–3X could be implementation advantage unrelated to speculation. A $\gamma=0$ run of their own stack vs. T5X is the obvious control and is absent.

W3. The i.i.d. assumption behind Eq. 1 is known to be false and its impact is never quantified. main.tex:249 flags it as "simplifying," and main.tex:438 notes "the $\beta$s aren't constant," but the paper never reports the empirical distribution of tokens-per-iteration, autocorrelation of acceptance, or a corrected prediction (e.g., a Markov model of $\beta$). Since this assumption drives every theorem and the theory-vs-empirics comparison, its error budget should be measured, not acknowledged.

W4. Internal contradiction about $c$. main.tex:328 states "$c$ was always less than $0.05$ and often negligibly close to 0," but Table 4 (main.tex:714,717,721,724) lists $c = 0.11$ for T5-large (250M vs 11B — "a couple of orders of magnitude smaller" by the paper's own phrasing). One of these is wrong.

W5. Reproducibility is weak. No code is released. The setup (main.tex:470) says "a single TPU-v4" — chip count, mesh topology, and sharding are unspecified; the T5X baseline version/configuration is unspecified; input lengths, number of examples, warmup, and timing methodology are unspecified; the $\alpha$ protocol (main.tex:513, "10K tokens generated by $M_p$") does not state which split of WMT EnDe / CNNDM. The GPT-like 97M model is trained by the authors but no training details (steps, LR, perplexity) are given (main.tex:515), and LaMDA (main.tex:517) is internal and cannot be checked at all. Only the T5 rows are even partially replicable by a reader.

W6. LaMDA and GPT-like "experiments" measure only $\alpha$; no walltime is reported. The intro (main.tex:185) says "We analyze our method in a variety of tasks ... and a dialog task with a 137B parameter LaMDA model," which reads as if the method was run at LaMDA scale. In fact Tables 3's LaMDA/GPT rows are $\alpha$ estimates only; the method was implemented and timed only for T5. The claim is defensible but the framing oversells coverage.

W7. No empirical comparison against the closest prior methods. Blockwise Parallel Decoding and SAD are compared only qualitatively (main.tex:594-598). A head-to-head (even re-implemented) on T5 translation would substantiate the novelty claim and quantify what exactness costs in speed.

W8. Scope of evaluation is narrow. Batch size 1 only (main.tex:470); no throughput/batched-serving analysis, which is where production decoding actually lives and where speculative decoding interacts awkwardly with batching (per-sequence variable acceptance). No discussion of black-box settings where full $p(x)$ distributions are unavailable (the method needs full logits).

Minor

  • Notation bug in Algorithm 1 (main.tex:226): the acceptance test $r_i > \frac{p_i(x)}{q_i(x)}$ never specifies that the ratio is evaluated at the sampled token $x_i$; as written, $p_i(x)/q_i(x)$ is a free variable. Should be $p_i(x_i)/q_i(x_i)$.
  • Citation error (main.tex:589): "Wisdom of Committees \citep{Matching_Model_and_Instance_Complexities}" attributes Wang et al.'s Wisdom of Committees to Schwartz et al. The correct bib entry Wisdom_of_Committees exists at references.bib:139-144 but is never cited.
  • Corollary proof is not rigorous (main.tex:347-349): "If we get an improvement for $\gamma$, we'd also get an improvement for any $0 < \gamma^* < \gamma$" is asserted, not proved; $g(\gamma)=\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{(1-\alpha)(\gamma c+1)}$ is not obviously unimodal. The result itself follows directly from evaluating $\gamma=1$ (which the proof then does), so the fix is to delete the unproven sentence.
  • Unproven claims: (a) main.tex:373: decoder ops "can be bounded from above by a single run of the same-size Transformer encoder" — no proof, no definition of the comparison basis; (b) main.tex:439: oracle improvement "up to ~60% higher" — no derivation.
  • $D_{LK}$ is the total variation distance ($\sum_x |p - \tfrac{p+q}{2}| = \tfrac12|p-q|_1$). main.tex:267-272 presents it as a new "natural divergence" without noting it is TV distance; this matters for the novelty framing of the analysis section.
  • Lenience (Appendix E) is under-evaluated. The 2.5X–5X factors (main.tex:753) are computed from the theory with an assumed $c=0.015$, never measured; and no output-quality metric (BLEU/ROUGE, diversity, KL to $p$) is reported for any $l<1$. The one-sided guarantee ($\le p(x)/l$, main.tex:745) says nothing about how much quality degrades. As written this section is a promise, not a result.
  • Beam search (Appendix D, main.tex:737-739) is a sketch. The condition $top_w(M_p) \subseteq top_u(M_q)$ per step is not obviously sufficient for identical beam search (beams are pruned across steps; the union of candidates must contain the true beam at every step). The authors themselves defer analysis — fine, but then it should not be listed as an "application."
  • Figure 1 caption says 38 tokens (main.tex:155); the attached figure1.png shows 32 visible tokens in the final row (including [START]/[END]). If subword tokens are collapsed in rendering, the count should match the rendering.
  • Typesetting/source hygiene: placeholder short caption \caption[test] (main.tex:155); \appendix issued twice (main.tex:638,641); footnote hack \stepcounter{footnote} (main.tex:524) with the corresponding \footnotetext placed in Related Work (main.tex:602), so the LaMDA $Top_{40}$ footnote can land on an unrelated page; labels with spaces (tbl:empirical alphas, main.tex:528); \vspace{1.04cm} hack (main.tex:460); camera-ready \usepackage[accepted]{icml2023} with named authors (main.tex:25,115-117) — if this is the reviewed version, it is not anonymized.
  • Typos: "anaylsis" (main.tex:320), "autogreressive" (main.tex:456), "sparcification" (main.tex:588), "CCN/DM" for CNNDM (main.tex:470), "speedup inference" (main.tex:608).
  • Unused/duplicate bib entries: gad (references.bib:41), iad_maybe (duplicate of sad, references.bib:49-55), Wisdom_of_Committees (references.bib:139), graves-adaptive-computation-for-rnn (references.bib:242) — none appear in main.bbl.
  • $\gamma$ selection procedure unstated: Table 1 rows use different $\gamma$ (7,5,3); were these grid-searched per row, or taken from Fig. 3? Affects whether Table 1 is a fair comparison across $M_q$ sizes.

4. Questions for the authors

  1. What is the measured walltime of one parallel $M_p$ evaluation over $\gamma+1$ positions as a function of $\gamma$ on your TPU-v4 setup? Where does the "no walltime increase" assumption (main.tex:320) break?
  2. What is the walltime of your implementation with speculation disabled ($\gamma=0$) vs. T5X? How much of Table 1 is implementation delta?
  3. Please report the empirical distribution (and autocorrelation) of tokens-per-iteration vs. the capped-geometric prediction of Eq. 1. How much of the T5-large theory gap (2.5 vs 1.7) is i.i.d.-error vs. parallel-cost error?
  4. main.tex:328 says $c<0.05$ always; Table 4 says $c=0.11$ for T5-large. Which is correct?
  5. Were the $\gamma$ values in Table 1 tuned per row? What is the sensitivity of the reported speedups to $\gamma$ (one row re-run at $\gamma\pm2$ would suffice)?
  6. Exact hardware/software configuration: TPU-v4 topology, sharding, T5X version, batch-1 timing protocol (examples, warmup, percentiles)? Which test split and how many sequences for the 10K-token $\alpha$ estimates?
  7. For lenience: measured walltimes and BLEU/ROUGE (or KL to $p$) at $l \in {0.5, 0.3, 0.1}$?
  8. How does speculative decoding behave at batch size > 1 (throughput serving)? Have you measured it?
  9. Algorithm 1, line 226: please confirm the acceptance ratio is evaluated at the sampled token $x_i$.
  10. Figure 1: is the 38-token count over subwords? The rendered figure shows 32 tokens.
  11. Can the GPT-like 97M model (architecture given at main.tex:515) and its $\alpha$ results be released or reproduced?

5. Assessment

Novelty: 7/10. The exactness-preserving stochastic speculative-sampling step is a genuinely new and elegant ingredient relative to Blockwise Parallel Decoding and SAD, and the formalization of "stochastic speculative execution" is a nice framing. However, the pieces are individually well known: parallel draft-and-verify is Stern et al. 2018; the residual-coupling trick $\min(p,q)$ is standard probability machinery; $D_{LK}$ is total variation distance; and Chen et al. 2023 arrived at the same algorithm independently and concurrently. The contribution is the combination, the exactness guarantee, and the analysis — solid but not deep.

Soundness: 6/10. The correctness proof (Appendix A) is fully rigorous and the theorems' algebra checks out (I verified Thms. 1–3, Eq. 1, the bigram example, and Table 2 numerically). But the empirical soundness is weaker: the free-parallelism assumption is unmeasured (W1), the baseline control is missing (W2), the i.i.d. error budget is unquantified (W3), and there is an internal contradiction about $c$ (W4). The headline 2X–3X claim survives these concerns (theory and experiment agree for the best configurations), but the mechanism attribution is not airtight.

Clarity: 8/10. The paper is exceptionally readable; the overview→algorithm→analysis→experiments flow is clean, figures match the text, and the theory is elementary and self-contained. Deductions: the Algorithm 1 notation bug, the misplaced LaMDA footnote, the oversold LaMDA coverage in the intro, and assorted typos.

Reproducibility: 4/10. No code, no hardware/sharding/baseline configuration, no timing protocol, no training details for the 97M model, LaMDA results uncheckable. A determined reader can approximately replicate the T5 $\alpha$ numbers from public checkpoints, but not the walltimes.

6. Abstract-claim audit

Abstract claim (main.tex:143-151) Evidence Verdict
"sample ... faster without any changes to the outputs" Appendix A proof gives distributional identity. For temp=0 outputs are identical; for temp=1 a run produces a different sample from the same distribution, not the same tokens as a seeded baseline run. Supported, with imprecise wording. "Without changing the output distribution" would be exact; "identical outputs" (also main.tex:150) is loose for temp>0.
"computing several tokens in parallel" Algorithm 1; Table 1; trace diagram. Supported.
"hard tasks include easier subtasks approximated by efficient models" Table 3: $\alpha \in [0.5, 0.9]$ for two-orders-of-magnitude-smaller $M_q$; bigram $\alpha=0.2$. Supported.
"running them in parallel on the outputs of the approximation models ... without changing the distribution" Correctness proof; but the walltime benefit depends on the unmeasured free-parallelism assumption (main.tex:320). Supported for the distribution claim; the speed side is supported empirically but not mechanistically isolated.
"accelerate existing off-the-shelf models without retraining or architecture changes" Existing T5 checkpoints used (main.tex:470). Supported.
"on T5-XXL ... 2X-3X acceleration compared to the standard T5X implementation, with identical outputs" Table 1: best-$M_q$ rows give 2.3X–3.4X (temp 0/1). The 3.4X row exceeds the stated range; T5-large rows (1.4X–1.7X) fall below it, but the abstract implicitly refers to the chosen best configuration. "Identical outputs" as above. Supported for the headline configuration, though the range is slightly misstated (should be ~2X–3.4X) and the baseline-control confound (W2) is unaddressed.

7. Recommendation

Accept. The paper presents a correct, simple, provably exact algorithm with a clean analysis and a real, measured 2X–3X end-to-end speedup on a production-grade stack — a contribution of unusual practical immediacy. The weaknesses (unmeasured parallel-cost assumption, missing $\gamma=0$ control, unquantified i.i.d. error, weak reproducibility, the $c<0.05$ contradiction, and the citation/notation errors above) are fixable in a revision and do not undermine the central claim, which is corroborated by two independent lines of evidence (theory and walltime) agreeing on the best configurations.

Confidence: 4 / 5. I verified all proofs and the theory–table numerics myself and inspected all figures; I cannot verify the T5X baseline setup or LaMDA numbers from the artifact, which is the main reason this is not a 5.

Scores: Novelty 7/10 · Soundness 6/10 · Clarity 8/10 · Reproducibility 4/10 · Overall: Accept (strong-accept borderline, contingent on W1/W2/W4 being addressed in the author response).
