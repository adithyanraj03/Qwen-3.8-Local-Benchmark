Peer Review: "Fast Inference from Transformers via Speculative Decoding"

Venue assumption: ICML (main track). Paper version reviewed: camera-ready source (main.tex, 802 lines, compiled with icml2023.sty, [accepted] option at main.tex:25).

1. Summary of contributions

The paper proposes speculative decoding: accelerate autoregressive decoding from a target model $M_p$ by having a cheap approximation model $M_q$ draft $\gamma$ tokens autoregressively, then verifying all $\gamma$ guesses plus one extra position with a single parallel forward pass of $M_p$. The core technical ingredient is speculative sampling (Sec. 2.3, Alg. 1): accept a draft token $x \sim q$ with probability $\min(1, p(x)/q(x))$; on rejection, resample from the normalized residual $\mathrm{norm}(\max(0, p-q))$. This provably yields samples exactly from $p$ (App. A). The paper derives closed forms for expected tokens per target-model call (Eq. 1), walltime improvement (Thm. 3.2), and increase in arithmetic operations (Thm. 3.4), relates acceptance to a divergence $D_{LK}$ (Thm. 3.1), and measures 2.3X–3.4X walltime speedups for T5-XXL vs. T5X at batch size 1 on TPU-v4 (Table 2), plus acceptance rates $\alpha$ for GPT-like 97M, T5-XXL, and LaMDA 137B pairs (Table 3).

Stated contributions (main.tex:190): (1) speculative execution generalized to the stochastic setting via speculative sampling; (2) a lossless decoding mechanism requiring no retraining, no architecture change, and no change to the output distribution.

2. Strengths

  1. The central algorithm is simple, correct, and genuinely useful. The correctness proof (main.tex:649-677) is short and complete; I verified the algebra. The property "identical distribution, no retraining, off-the-shelf checkpoints" (Table 2 uses public T5 checkpoints, main.tex:470) is a real and previously unmet niche relative to Blockwise Parallel Decoding (greedy-only, extra training) and SAD (copy heuristic, greedy-only).
  2. Theory and experiment are quantitatively connected, and mostly agree. I recomputed every number in Table 1 (main.tex:402-413) from Eqs. 1 and Thm. 3.4: all six rows are exact (e.g., $\alpha=0.9,\gamma=10$: speed $6.86$, ops $1.60$). In Table 5 (main.tex:710-724$) 9 of 12 "Exp" values match the formula from the stated $(\alpha,\gamma,c)$ to rounding. The bigram 1.25X claim (main.tex:450) checks out exactly ($(1-0.2^4)/0.8 = 1.248$). The figures (alpha_v_tokens.pdf,alpha_v_optimal_gamma.pdf,arith_ops.pdf`) are consistent with the formulas (e.g., $\alpha=0.75,\gamma=7 \Rightarrow 3.6$ tokens, $2.2\times$ ops; optimal $\gamma \approx 9$ at $\alpha=0.75, c=0.02$).
  3. Honest scoping of limitations in the Discussion (main.tex:606-608): the concurrency-for-operations trade-off is stated plainly, and the arithmetic-ops analysis (Thm. 3.4) is included precisely because the walltime model hides it.
  4. Broad $\alpha$ evidence across three model families and 1.5 orders of magnitude of size ratios (Table 3), including the useful negative-ish result that unigram/bigram drafts give $\alpha \le 0.23$ — this calibrates expectations for practitioners.
  5. The comparison to plain rejection sampling (App. B, main.tex:681-689) preempts the obvious "isn't this just rejection sampling?" objection, and the inequality chain there is correct.
  6. Writing is, on the whole, exceptionally clear; the overview in Sec. 2.1 could be taught as-is.

3. Weaknesses

  1. The walltime model's key assumption is strong and only validated in its most favorable regime. Thm. 3.2 assumes "$\gamma+1$ concurrent evaluations of $M_p$ in parallel without increasing the walltime" (main.tex:320). All experiments are batch size 1 on a single TPU-v4 (main.tex:470). Production serving is throughput-oriented at batch $b>1$, where speculative decoding multiplies the effective batch by $\gamma+1$ and the free-parallelism assumption fails; the abstract's "2X-3X" therefore does not characterize the method's operating envelope. No throughput experiment, no compute-saturation analysis, no GPU numbers.
  2. The i.i.d.-$\beta$ assumption is acknowledged but never quantified, and one row suggests it matters a lot. Table 5: EnDe, T5-large, t=0 predicts 3.3X, measures 1.7X — a 48% miss, which the paper attributes vaguely to "implementation details" and the i.i.d. assumption (main.tex:699). No acceptance-autocorrelation measurement, no per-position $\beta$ statistics, nothing that lets a reader predict when the theory will fail.
  3. $\alpha$ is estimated on prefixes generated by $M_p$ itself (main.tex:513), but during speculative decoding the accepted prefixes are a mixture of $M_q$-drafted and $M_p$-corrected continuations. The prefix distribution the algorithm actually visits is not the one $\alpha$ is measured on; the theory silently assumes they coincide.
  4. Reproducibility is weak. No code release is mentioned anywhere; the implementation is internal; walltime methodology (warmup, repetitions, variance, how T5X was configured) is absent; $c$ values are "estimated based on profiler traces" (main.tex:697) and not derivable by a reader; the GPT-like 97M and LaMDA models are not public. The only externally reproducible result (T5-XXL) is exactly the one whose implementation is not released. No error bars on any number in the paper.
  5. Two appendix claims do not reproduce from the paper's own formulas. (a) Lenience speedups "2.5X, 3.1X, 3.6X, and 5X" (main.tex:753) with stated $c=0.015$ and the $\alpha$ values of Table 6: maximizing Thm. 3.2 over $\gamma$ gives 2.33/2.94/3.45/4.79 — every claimed value is ~0.2X too high, and the $\gamma$ used is never stated. (b) "up to ~60% higher" oracle improvement (main.tex:439-440) is asserted without derivation; for typical $\alpha \in [0.6, 0.8], c=0.02$ I compute 15–31%; ~60% only holds near $\alpha \approx 0.9$. (The temperature-0 lenience numbers at main.tex:779 do check out: 3.3/3.3/3.9/4.9 for $\gamma=8, c=0.015$.)
  6. $D_{LK}$ is presented as a new divergence but is exactly total variation distance. $\sum_x |p(x)-M(x)| = \tfrac12\sum_x|p(x)-q(x)|$ (main.tex:271-281); the "natural divergence $D_{LK}$" and its properties (Cor. 3.1) are textbook TV. Naming it after the authors without citing the standard name overstates the conceptual novelty of Sec. 3.2.
  7. Related-work citation errors. main.tex:589 attributes "Wisdom of Committees" to \citep{Matching_Model_and_Instance_Complexities} (Schwartz et al., "The Right Tool for the Job"); the actual Wisdom of Committees entry (references.bib:139-144) is never cited. Worse, GAD — Xia et al., "Lossless Speedup of Autoregressive Translation with Generalized Aggressive Decoding" (references.bib:41-47) — is in the bib but never cited or discussed, even though it is concurrent lossless parallel-decoding work directly in the space the paper claims (the compiled main.bbl confirms it is absent from the reference list). iad_maybe and graves-adaptive-computation-for-rnn are also dead bib entries.
  8. Scope gaps relative to the "good default" claim (main.tex:188). Beam search gets a one-paragraph sketch with no experiments (App. D); LaMDA 137B and GPT-like 97B experiments report only $\alpha$, never walltime (main.tex:510-520), so the 137B "variety" advertised in the intro (main.tex:185) is not an end-to-end result; no comparison of walltime against Blockwise Parallel Decoding or SAD on a shared task.
  9. The "identical outputs" phrasing is loose for stochastic sampling. For temp=1 the guarantee is distributional; a temp=1 run does not return the same token sequence a baseline sampler would (different RNG consumption). The abstract says "identical outputs" (main.tex:150) where the body correctly says "same distribution" (main.tex:181). Also, the exactness of the accept/reject test depends on floating-point agreement between the $p$ and $q$ pipelines; precision is never discussed.

4. Questions for the authors

  1. What is the speedup at batch sizes 4/8/32, or in tokens/sec throughput? Where does the "γ+1 parallel runs are free" assumption (main.tex:320) break, and can you give a diagnostic (FLOPs/bandwidth ratio) for when speculative decoding stops paying?
  2. For Table 5's EnDe/T5-large/t=0 row (3.3 predicted vs. 1.7 measured): how much of the gap is i.i.d.-$\beta$ violation vs. verification cost not captured by $c$? Please report per-position acceptance rates and their autocorrelation.
  3. How exactly were walltimes measured (warmup, number of runs, std)? Will the T5X-based implementation be released?
  4. Lenience, temp=1 (main.tex:753): which $\gamma$ produced 2.5/3.1/3.6/5X? With $c=0.015$ and Table 6's $\alpha$, the formula's optimum is 2.33/2.94/3.45/4.79.
  5. "~60% higher" oracle gain (main.tex:440): over which $(\alpha, c)$ grid? I get 15–31% for $\alpha\in[0.6,0.8], c=0.02$.
  6. Does effective $\alpha$ drift during decoding, given drafts change the prefix distribution relative to the $M_p$-generated prefixes used to measure $\alpha$ (main.tex:513)?
  7. main.tex:373: "total number of arithmetic operations ... can be bounded from above by a single run of the same-size Transformer encoder" — please prove this; it seems to ignore attention over the cached prefix for each of the $\gamma+1$ positions.
  8. Figure 1 / main.tex:182: the text says 38 tokens in 9 runs; the final line of figure1.png shows ~31 tokens (incl. [END]) across 9 lines. Please reconcile or state the tokenization.
  9. main.tex:589: is the intended citation Schwartz et al. or Wang et al. (Wisdom of Committees)? And why is GAD (Xia et al. 2022, in your own bib) not discussed?
  10. In what sense are temp=1 outputs "identical" (main.tex:150)? Do you claim bit-identical sequences under matched RNG, or only distributional identity?
  11. What precision are $p$, $q$, and the $r_i$ comparisons computed in, and does bf16 rounding perturb the exactness guarantee in practice?
  12. App. D (beam search): any empirical numbers for the $top_w(M_p) \subseteq top_u(M_q)$ procedure, even on one task?

5. Assessment

Novelty: 7/10. The sampling trick (accept $\min(1,p/q)$, resample from normalized residual) is mathematically elementary — it is rejection sampling with residual resampling, and $D_{LK}$ is TV distance — but I am not aware of it being used for lossless speculative decoding with parallel verification before this work and the concurrent Chen et al. 2023 (main.tex:600). The real novelty is the systems framing: stochastic speculative execution, the $\alpha$/$c$/$\gamma$ performance model, and the demonstration that off-the-shelf checkpoints give 2–3X for free. The delta over Stern et al. 2018 and SAD (stochastic setting + exactness + no training) is real and clearly articulated (main.tex:594-598).

Soundness: 7/10. All derivations I checked are correct (App. A; Thms. 3.2/3.4; Table 1; App. B; the temp=0 lenience numbers). Deductions: the free-parallelism assumption is load-bearing and under-probed (W1); i.i.d. $\beta$ is unquantified where it visibly fails (W2); prefix-distribution shift in $\alpha$ estimation (W3); two appendix claims don't reproduce from stated parameters (W5); the monotonicity step in Cor. 3.1's proof (main.tex:348) is asserted, not shown (the result is true — $f(\gamma)$ is unimodal — but the proof as written is a gap).

Clarity: 8/10. Best-written aspect of the paper is Secs. 2–3. Deductions for: the abstract's "identical outputs" ambiguity (W9), the unproven ~60% and encoder-bound claims stated as facts, the misplaced Top-40 footnote (the \stepcounter{footnote}/\footnotetext hack at main.tex:524,602 attaches a LaMDA-experiments footnote to the Related Work page), and typos: "anaylsis" (main.tex:320), "autogreressive" (main.tex:456), "sparcification" (main.tex:588), "CCN/DM" for CNN/DM (main.tex:470).

Reproducibility: 4/10. Public T5 checkpoints and a precise algorithm are a good base, but: no code, no timing protocol, no variance, non-public GPT-like/LaMDA models, $c$ estimated from unpublished profiler traces, and the lenience numbers not derivable. A reader can reproduce the $\alpha$ values and the figures, but not Table 2.

7. Are the abstract's claims supported by the experiments?

Abstract claim (lines 144-151) Verdict
"sample ... faster without any changes to the outputs" Supported as distributional identity (proof, App. A). For temp=1, "outputs" should read "output distribution" (W9).
"by computing several tokens in parallel" Supported algorithmically; the walltime realization is validated only at batch size 1 on TPU-v4.
"can accelerate existing off-the-shelf models without retraining or architecture changes" Supported — public T5 checkpoints, no training (main.tex:470).
"demonstrate it on T5-XXL and show a 2X-3X acceleration compared to the standard T5X implementation, with identical outputs" Supported within scope: Table 2 shows 2.3X–3.4X on EnDe/CNNDM, batch 1, single TPU-v4. The abstract omits the batch-size-1 and hardware scope, and "identical outputs" is exact only for argmax. The intro's LaMDA/GPT-like "variety" (main.tex:185) is $\alpha$-only, not walltime — fine, since the abstract only claims T5-XXL.

Net: the headline number is honest but under-scoped; the strongest qualifier missing from the abstract is "at batch size 1".

8. Recommendation

Accept (weak accept, 6/10). Confidence: 4/5.

Rationale: the contribution is correct, clearly presented, immediately deployable, and quantitatively validated where it claims to be (T5-XXL); the theory-experiment agreement is unusually good for a systems-flavored ML paper, with the exceptions catalogued above. The weaknesses are real but are matters of scope and reporting (batch size >1, beam search, variance, code release, two unreproducible appendix numbers, a mis-citation and an uncited concurrent lossless method) rather than of correctness, and none undermines the central claim. I would push the meta-reviewer to require, in the rebuttal/camera-ready: (i) a batch-size/throughput data point or an explicit scope statement in the abstract, (ii) corrected lenience numbers, (iii) the GAD discussion and the Wisdom-of-Committees citation fix, and (iv) a code release commitment.
